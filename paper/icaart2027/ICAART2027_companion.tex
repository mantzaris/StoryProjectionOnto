\section*{1. Reading the networks}
The figures show which relationships the model extracted, how they relate to a reader's question, and where errors remain. They use the same outputs, reference answers and assessments reported in the main paper. No additional experiments were performed.

\textbf{The two approaches.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Pre-extract/select (A)} first extracts relationships without seeing the question. Fixed filters then select records for that question.
\item \textbf{Contextual extraction (B)} receives the text and question together and generates its own relationships.
\end{itemize}
A and B are alternative approaches. B does not modify or continue A's graph.

\textbf{Reading an arrow.}
Each arrow runs from the subject to the object. Its label describes their relationship and identifies the supporting text. Labels also show any recorded time interval or attribution, such as whose belief is represented. A believer label describes the existing relationship and does not add another extracted link. In the figures, underscores appear as spaces and \texttt{loc} means \texttt{located\_in}.

Within each panel, exactly matching names share a node. Different names remain separate, even when they might refer to the same entity. Multiple records connecting the same nodes remain separate arrows. Omitted time or attribution fields are \texttt{null}, meaning unspecified. A missing time interval does not mean that a relationship holds forever.

\textbf{Understanding the assessment labels.}
Table~\ref{tab:supp-assessment-legend} explains the assessment symbols.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Assessment symbols used in the network figures.}
\label{tab:supp-assessment-legend}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
$+$         & Meaning supported by the text \\
$\sim$      & Meaning partly supported \\
\texttt{x}  & Meaning unsupported \\
?           & Interpretation unresolved \\
\texttt{I}  & Supported, but outside the question's scope \\
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Symbols and line styles accompany the colours, so colour alone is not needed to read the assessments.

In Orchard, node colours distinguish people, objects and places. These categories were added for display and were not inferred by the model. The broad extraction shown in the main paper has no assessment labels. It displays the model's output rather than a verified reference answer.

\section*{6. Interpreting agreement and preserved meaning}
\label{sec:supp-meaning}
Two fable examples illustrate why strict agreement and semantic coverage are assessed separately.

\textbf{Preserved meaning.}
The record \PassiveExample\ correctly expresses the capture described in S4. It fails strict matching because the frozen reference alternatives do not include this passive wording. The semantic assessment therefore identifies useful information that the strict score does not credit.

\textbf{Partial meaning.}
The record \PartialExample\ captures the Mouse's movement over the Lion, but S1 specifically describes movement over his face. The actor and broad action are preserved, while the more precise endpoint is omitted. This receives partial support rather than complete target coverage.

These examples demonstrate the value of inspecting network relationships alongside their source passages. Readers can distinguish alternative wording from missing detail while examining the original outputs and unchanged scores.
