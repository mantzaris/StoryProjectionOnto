## Abstract (164 whitespace-delimited words, citation included)

Readers need different views of the same narrative, but extracted graphs can obscure errors in participants, time and attribution. We present an implemented prototype for compact, evidence-linked relationship extraction and visual inspection. We compare query-blind extraction followed by fixed selection with independent contextual extraction on three synthetic stories and three short published passages. Strict agreement is separated from semantic preservation, relevance and unresolved interpretation using Codex-authored references and assessments. All 12 story responses were parseable; fixed selection matched every possession and interval-location target without extras, whereas contextual answers retained irrelevant facts. On fable actions, strict F1 was 0.625 for selection and 0.308 for contextual extraction. Alice's contextual claims preserved 3 of 4 semantic targets despite zero strict F1. Prose parsing succeeded in 7 of 9 calls. These results demonstrate inspectable useful and partial content alongside substantive errors. The contribution is an empirical work-in-progress comparison and source-linked inspection workflow, not evidence of general reliability or contextual superiority.
